    5 How we keep the words accurate

    • With a connected online helper, audio is preferred. Captions alone may be incomplete or incorrect.
    • Auto language reads the spoken-language track itself, then cross-checks the script of the words.
    • Know the language? Pick it. A picked language is always surer than Auto.
    • Enhanced audio accuracy uses two word-timed audio passes when supported; Groq uses Large V3 and Turbo.
    • The first pass can use the video title as a spelling hint; the second pass omits it.
    • AI compares bounded alternatives from the audio passes. It cannot invent a third alternative or verify lyrics from memory. Use Align reference for an authorized performance transcript.
    • Real repeated words are kept, not shrunk — if the singer really sings a word twice in a row, both stay. Only a repetition that is an echo from the captions is kept once.
    • The text model cannot hear audio; unresolved or unsafe alternatives retain the primary result.
    • The result reports matching anchors and changed bounded differences; neither count guarantees correctness.
    • The AI review processes disagreements in small batches. File and provider limits still apply.

    6 Good to know

    • YouTube’s auto captions can still get names and fast lines wrong.
    • Singing over music is the hardest part. A misheard sung word can stay wrong.
    • A web page cannot pull the sound out of a YouTube video by itself.
    • With the free worker set up, the link alone is enough — the worker fetches the sound and your provider writes the words.
    • Online, your provider listens to a file you pick.
    • Offline, the pack fetches the sound for you.
    • A page opened by double-clicking the file can’t connect any key. Open it through its web address instead — start_html.bat in the pack does that for you.
    • The words belong to the video’s maker. Keep them for your own use.
